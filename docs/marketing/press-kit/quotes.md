## On the product thesis

> "Most people die with a thousand small secrets nobody can find. We
> don't think that's a tech problem to solve with a better folder
> structure — it's a trust problem. So we built a vault that the right
> people can open, exactly when they need to, and never before."

## On zero-knowledge

> "Zero-knowledge isn't a marketing word for us. It's the entire premise.
> If we ever shipped a version that could read your vault, we'd be
> betraying the only reason customers chose us."

## On the 14-day hold

> "Most security products optimise for speed. The release engine
> optimises for second-guessing. Fourteen days, four channels, one-tap
> abort — because the cost of being too slow is a delayed grief, and the
> cost of being too fast is an unsurvivable mistake."

## On nominee KYC

> "We don't ask your nominee for a copy of their Aadhaar. The trust
> comes from the three out of five people you already chose. Document
> theatre at the worst week of someone's life is a failure of design,
> not a security feature."

## On bootstrapping

> "We're bootstrap by choice, not by accident. A vault product has to
> survive its company. VCs push for growth that compromises that. The
> only way to align my incentives with my users for the next ten years
> is to not have a board to answer to."

## On the launch test

> "Before we let anyone else trust us with the records their family will
> need after they're gone, we ran the death-recovery test on ourselves,
> in public, end to end. If the test had failed, we wouldn't be opening
> signups. It didn't. So we are."

## On India + global

> "Lyfos is built in India because India is the most demanding place to
> build for this problem — fragmented banks, multi-language families,
> messy paper trails. If it works here, it works anywhere. We start in
> India and expand from there."

## On what we're not

> "We are not a will-writing service. We are not a probate platform. We
> are not a digital legacy social network. We are a vault. The product
> is one screen, one passphrase, and a release engine. Everything else
> is somebody else's product."
